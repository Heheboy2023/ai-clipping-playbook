# Record your first practice source

No client footage yet? Make a small recording on your phone. This exercise supplies a real face, voice, pauses, and a story to edit in Chapters 1–2 and 7–10 of *Clip It & Cash In*.

## Record

1. Set the phone sideways on a steady surface. Keep your face in the middle.
2. Face a soft light. Reduce fan noise and other sounds nearby.
3. Read the script below at your normal pace. Leave a pause before and after it.
4. Play it back. Check that the speech is clear.
5. Transfer the original video to your computer. Keep its original extension.
6. Name it `practice-story.mp4` or `practice-story.mov`, matching the actual format. Do not turn MOV into MP4 by renaming the extension.

## Original practice script

This story is made up for practice. It is not a claim about anyone's results.

> Okay, here's the mistake. I spent an hour making an animated intro for a thirty-second clip. The actual story didn't start until eight seconds in. Then I watched it on my phone. I was already reaching for the next video before my own story started. So I removed the intro. The clip opened with the mistake instead. Same recording. Same idea. Much faster start. My new rule is simple: get to the reason someone clicked.

## Edit

- Start at “I spent an hour.”
- Keep enough of the story to explain the change.
- End after the last useful line, before the trailing silence.
- Use a vertical 1080 × 1920 timeline, center the speaker, and add one modest punch-in.
- Type captions after cutting, so they match the finished edit.
- Export with visible subtitles burned into the picture.

Use your own recording's timing. No pre-timed SRT is supplied because speaking speed varies. The book teaches manual captions first, then transcription later.

## What the other sample files contain

`examples/fixtures/sample-podcast.mp4` and the other generated test videos contain patterns and tones. They test cutting, resolution, captions, and batch code. They do not contain the dialogue in the sample transcript, and they cannot prove speech-transcription accuracy, speaker detection, or face tracking.

Keep personal recordings in `work/` or your separate editing folder, never in the public repository. You can use this original practice script in your own recordings.
